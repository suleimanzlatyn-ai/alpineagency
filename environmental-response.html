Incident readiness

Ready to respond. Clear on the next step.

Environmental incidents require safety-first decisions, established site procedures and the appropriate emergency or specialist responders.

Discuss your requirement ↗All services

Preparedness begins before an incident.

Site teams can prepare by identifying hazards, escalation contacts, containment resources, reporting responsibilities and arrangements for specialist support. Actual response capability, operating hours and coverage must be confirmed directly with the responsible provider.

If an incident is happening now, protect people first, follow the site emergency plan and contact the appropriate local emergency services and authorities. Do not enter unsafe areas or attempt specialist clean-up without training and suitable equipment.

Questions to consider

  • Site emergency plan and emergency contact list.
  • Spill, fire, flood and pollution scenarios relevant to the site.
  • Personnel roles, access routes and communication protocols.
  • Appropriate containment resources and waste handling arrangements.
  • Incident records, reporting duties and post-incident review.

Formal assessments, regulated work, specialist engineering and emergency services should only be undertaken by suitably qualified and authorised professionals. Confirm Alpine’s specific scope and qualifications before engagement.
