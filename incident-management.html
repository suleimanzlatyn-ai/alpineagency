Environmental Incident Management

Prepare for and manage environmental incidents such as oil spills, chemical releases and hazardous-site events.

Discuss this service ↗All services

When pollution occurs, prompt decisions can help limit harm to people, land, water and operations. Alpine supports a structured approach to incident preparedness and management, with clear roles, communication and follow-up actions appropriate to the situation.

How oil spills affect the ecosystem

Oil can coat shorelines and aquatic vegetation, reduce water quality, and expose fish, birds and other wildlife to harmful substances. Where a spill is suspected, the priority is to protect people, prevent further spread where safe and appropriate, and arrange assessment and response by competent specialists.

Incident types

Oil and fuel spills; chemical releases; suspected pollution of soil or water; hazardous-site concerns; uncontrolled discharges and other environmental events requiring assessment.

A structured response

Establish the situation and immediate safety priorities; identify the substance and potential pathways where safe to do so; contain or prevent further spread through appropriately qualified responders; coordinate reporting and specialist support; plan clean-up, remediation and monitoring as required.

Preparedness before an incident

Develop escalation contacts, incident checklists, reporting pathways and response plans. Emergency availability, field response capability and response times are confirmed explicitly rather than assumed.
